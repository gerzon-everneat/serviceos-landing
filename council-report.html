The Sceptic
Stress-tester
You are optimising for visual novelty when the actual conversion bottleneck is trust and credibility — and you've already stripped out the single highest-converting element (real testimonials) without a replacement strategy.
  • The target customer — a solo or 3-person cleaning or HVAC operator running jobs off their phone — does not browse software review sites or follow SaaS Twitter. They convert because a peer recommended it at a trade meetup or in a Facebook group for local service businesses. The landing page is usually step two, not step one. No hero section redesign changes that referral dynamic.
  • "No fake testimonials" is correctly honoured but its implication isn't faced: Jobber and Housecall Pro have hundreds of named customer reviews on G2 and Capterra. You've removed the #1 converting element in the category and are compensating with aesthetics — that's a substitution problem, not a creative brief.
  • Lime on #060606 reads "design agency / crypto startup" to a 45–55 year-old HVAC or cleaning business owner on a Samsung mid-range. Housecall Pro uses plain white with orange accents. Jobber uses sky blue and white. Both brands look exactly like the SMB owner's expectation of "software I can trust." The dark premium aesthetic may be quietly filtering out the actual ICP — and attracting younger, design-aware visitors who are not the buyer.
  • ServiceTitan occupies the enterprise/high-complexity end of the market (50+ employee orgs, $150k+ ARR deals); Workiz targets price-sensitive small operators who want the cheapest functional option. The whitespace neatr can credibly own is "serious software that doesn't require a 6-week onboarding and doesn't charge you until the job is done" — but none of the current variants say that explicitly to the 3-person cleaning crew owner.
● MEDIUM — Directionally strong; no funnel data to quantify
Favours audience-fit and trust-signal density over visual sophistication; may underweight brand perception and aspirational segment.

The Strategist
Long-horizon systems thinking
Stop testing aesthetics and start testing conviction — each variant should embody a single falsifiable belief about what makes a field service owner say "this was built for me."
  • v15–v23 tests presentation style, not belief systems. Lock the visual language; vary the psychological contract offered: autonomy, status, survival, growth, or belonging.
  • The five identity archetypes worth testing against the target customer: The Exhausted Founder (wants their evenings back), The Aspiring Operator (wants to feel like a real business, not a guy with a van), The Skeptic (tried software, got burned, almost doesn't want to try again), The Grinder (working 70 hrs/week, knows it's not sustainable), The Quiet Competitor (just wants to out-execute the other guys in the same zip code). Each requires a different first sentence.
  • The identity archetype that wins the first cohort of real customers defines brand positioning for 3 years — this is the highest-optionality decision available right now. Jobber won on "grow your business." Housecall Pro won on "run a professional operation." The owner of "run it without the chaos" is still unclaimed.
  • Competitive white space: ServiceTitan's positioning is "enterprise power" (complex, expensive, 50+ employee orgs). Workiz's is "affordable starter software." Neither owns "intelligent simplicity for the 1–10 person crew who can't afford to hire an office manager." That exact gap is neatr's to take — but only if the landing page makes a clear claim to it.
  • Without a conversion thesis per variant, adding more pages creates beautiful portfolio + weak feedback loop. Whichever identity angle wins dictates the product's entire go-to-market narrative against established players with large content moats.
● HIGH — Assumes measurement infrastructure is in place
Weights "who does this attract in year 3" more than "which headline gets more clicks this week." May underweight 90-day traction urgency.

The Operator
Execution reality
Build in a single focused sprint using five proven high-converting structures: ROI outcome specificity, credibility metrics wall, interactive workflow demo, niche-specific micro-page, and anti-software simplicity.
  • Clone best existing variant, swap hero angle, layer one new conversion mechanic per page — cuts build time to 2–4 hours each vs. starting from scratch.
  • The target customer's decision process is fast and emotional, not rational and comparative. They are not opening 4 tabs and reading pricing pages. They scan the hero, feel "yes this is for someone like me" or they don't, and they either fill the form or close the tab. The mechanic that works is the one that triggers recognition in under 6 seconds.
  • No-testimonial constraint is solvable: outcome metrics ("Jobs dispatched in beta: 847"), named-use-case vignettes ("Maria, 2-person cleaning crew, Miami — invoices sent in 4 mins"), industry logo strips (QuickBooks, Google Maps) signal legitimacy without fabricating customers. Housecall Pro uses "Join 40,000+ pros" — neatr can use the beta metric equivalent.
  • Niche-specific variant (separate copy for cleaning vs. HVAC vs. landscaping) is highest-ROI but highest-effort — budget 1–2 hours of dedicated copy work per niche angle. Target customer research shows cleaning operators are the most mobile-first, most skeptical of desktop software, and most likely to decide on the same day they see an ad. Start there.
  • On pricing: Jobber starts at $29/mo, Housecall Pro at $59/mo. neatr's "invoiced after job completes" model is structurally differentiated — that's not just a feature, it's a category-level threat to the subscription model. A cleaning business owner who lost $200 to Jobber before booking a single client will respond to this instantly. It belongs on the page as a positioning statement, not buried in FAQ.
● HIGH — Structurally well-understood patterns; no exotic dependencies
Defaults to what ships fastest over what looks most impressive. Will trade a polished animation for a working interactive element every time.

The Creative
Lateral reframer
Stop selling software features and start selling identity transformation — these owners don't want "automation," they want to finally feel like they built a real business instead of just owning a job.
  • "The Hired CEO" concept: show what a Tuesday looks like after neatr — morning coffee, 3 jobs dispatched, 2 invoices sent, crew assigned, all without the owner being involved. Copy: "You built this business. Now let it run." Competitors sell you a dashboard; neatr sells you your time back.
  • "The Competitor's Secret" angle: reframe neatr as the invisible infrastructure organised operators already use — FOMO through social proof by inference, not fake scarcity. Implied framing: "The cleaning businesses booking circles around you aren't working harder. They switched." No testimonials needed.
  • "Anti-Software arc" (v26–v28): lean into owner skepticism — "We know you've tried this before," "No contract. No upfront. Just results." as the hero, and "Built for people who hate Jobber" as the most direct radical-simplicity variant. Naming the incumbent lowers category education cost; the ICP already knows what Jobber is and likely has a grievance with it.
● HIGH — All existing v15–v23 variants still sell AT the owner; none sell WITH their identity and skepticism
Defaults to reframing audience self-concept over product features; weights emotional identity over rational value propositions; may underweight executional specificity.

Chairman's Synthesis

The Verdict

Synthesised from 5 council perspectives + peer review cross-examination

Core Tension

The council split along a clean fault line: D argues for faster execution of proven visual mechanics (ship more, test faster), while B, C, and E argue the landing page itself is not the constraining variable — trust deficit and identity misalignment are. Beneath that is a second tension A names correctly: there's zero empirical data on any existing variant, which means both camps are arguing from pattern-matching, not measurement. The real question isn't "which visual concept converts best?" — it's "which psychological contract does a field service owner say yes to?" Nobody has tested that yet.

There is also a competitive reality the council only gestures at: Jobber and Housecall Pro collectively own the awareness layer in this category. Every Google search for "field service software" or "scheduling app for cleaners" surfaces them first. neatr cannot out-spend them on intent keywords. The only viable path is to occupy a psychological position they cannot easily copy — neatr's payment model (invoiced after job completion, no subscription lock-in) is structurally unique. None of v24–v28 should go live without making that fact the sharpest competitive weapon on the page, not a footnote.

Top Options

OPTION 1 — RECOMMENDED

Identity-First Variants

Each of v24–v28 targets a distinct owner self-concept (C + E convergence). Same visual quality, different psychological contract. Lock the lime/dark design system; vary the conviction. Generates strategic signal, not just visual signal.

OPTION 2

Outcome-Specificity Variants

Lead every hero with a single concrete, measurable claim: "Recover 8 hours a week," "Get paid the day the job's done." Each variant pins one outcome, adds one new credibility mechanic (A + D convergence). Fastest to build and test.

OPTION 3

Niche Micro-Pages

One page per audience segment: cleaning, HVAC, landscaping, maintenance, window cleaning. Same structure, entirely specific copy and outcome claims per trade. Highest-effort but most directly testable via paid channel targeting.

Recommendation — Build These 5 Variants

Build identity-first variants that sell the transformation, not the software. Each page locks the visual system and varies only the psychological contract offered to the owner.

v24"The Hired CEO" — A day in the life after neatr. Morning coffee. 3 jobs dispatched. 2 invoices sent. Zero calls made. Copy: "You built this business. Now let it run." Dark theme, timeline visual, no dashboard.
v25"We know you've tried this before" — Leads with honesty and skepticism. Addresses the "burned by SaaS" objection head-on. Minimal white layout, almost editorial. The anti-hype page.
v26"No contract. No upfront. Just results." — Risk-reversal as the hero, not a footnote. The invoiced-after-completion model front and center. White theme, ultra-minimal, single CTA. Eliminates the biggest objection before it forms.
v27"The Competitor's Secret" — Reframes neatr as the invisible infrastructure the organised operators in your market already use. FOMO by inference, not fake scarcity. Copy: "The cleaners who are booking while you're quoting."
v28Niche-specific: Cleaning-only page — concrete outcome numbers, cleaning-specific pain copy, before/after for a cleaning business specifically. Tests whether audience specificity outperforms generic field-service framing.

Deal-Breakers — Cannot Be Ignored

  • Aesthetic mismatch risk (B): Lime on #060606 reads "design agency / crypto startup" to a 45+ trades owner on a mid-range Android. v25 and v26 (skepticism-first, risk-reversal) must be white-theme with maximum legibility — not cinematic dark.
  • Measurement gap (A): Without form conversion tracking on each variant, v24–v28 generate zero usable signal. Confirm Vercel Analytics or equivalent captures which variant's CTA receives submissions before building anything.
  • No testimonials = trust vacuum (B): Every identity variant must include a credibility substitute: outcome metrics, use-case vignettes, or tool logos (QuickBooks, Google Maps) — the page can't just make promises with nothing to anchor them.

Strongest Dissenting View

D's position deserves acknowledgment: all of this strategy is worthless if the pages don't ship. The fastest path to actual conversion data is to build something — even a mediocre page with good tracking beats a perfect page still being designed. The council's risk is analysis paralysis dressed as strategic rigour. If 90-day traction matters more than 3-year positioning, ignore Option 1, run Option 2 (outcome specificity), and start measuring immediately.

Implementation Blueprint — v24 to v28

v24

The Hired CEO

IDENTITY TRANSFORMATION · DAY-IN-THE-LIFE
"You built this business. Now let it run."
Hero shows a timeline of a Tuesday after neatr — 7:42am: booking received automatically, 7:43am: crew assigned by AI, 12:40pm: job done + invoice sent, 5:00pm: owner never touched their phone. No dashboard, no feature list. Just a calm day. Single lime CTA below.
TARGET: The Exhausted Founder — 40s, 6-person HVAC or cleaning crew, working evenings to keep up. Has tried to "delegate more" but systems keep breaking. Primary objection: "I'll still have to manage it."
Dark theme
v25

We Know You've Tried This

ANTI-HYPE · SKEPTICISM-FIRST · HONEST
"Most software promises a lot. We'd rather show you 30 seconds of what actually happens."
Opens by acknowledging the audience has been burned before. Single column, editorial layout, almost no UI chrome. Names the specific failures by name: "Jobber charged $200/mo before you booked your first job. Housecall Pro took a week to set up. Spreadsheets broke every time someone left." Then pivots: "Here's what we do differently — you pay nothing until a job is complete." Minimal animations, maximum credibility.
TARGET: The Skeptic — 35–50, solo operator or 2-person team, tried Jobber or a similar tool, cancelled after 2 months. Now back on WhatsApp and notes. Knows they need something but "not going through that again." Primary objection: "They all promise the same thing."
White theme — high legibility
v26

No Contract. No Upfront.

RISK-REVERSAL AS HERO · OBJECTION-FIRST
"You pay nothing until a job completes. That's it."
The invoiced-after-completion model as the entire hero — not buried in small print. Ultra-minimal layout: 3 lines of copy, one CTA, no screenshots. Below fold: quick "how it works" and the 3 credibility mechanics (outcome metrics, use-case vignettes, tool logos). Eliminates the biggest objection before it forms.
TARGET: The Grinder — 30s–40s, residential cleaning or landscaping, 1–4 crew. Cash-flow tight. Paying for Jobber felt like burning money while still figuring out the product. This owner wants zero financial risk before they see value. Primary objection: "I can't afford to pay for another tool that doesn't work out."
White — minimal, focused
v27

The Competitor's Secret

INFERENCE FOMO · SOCIAL PROOF BY IMPLICATION
"The cleaners who are booking while you're quoting. The landscapers who invoice before they leave the driveway."
No fake testimonials — uses inference instead. Positions neatr as the tool the organised, successful operators in your market already run on. Crucially: the "organised competitors" framing works without naming Jobber or Housecall Pro, because the ICP doesn't aspire to be a software user — they aspire to be the best operator in their area. No scarcity countdown, no fake urgency. Just the uncomfortable implication that someone in your zip code is already a step ahead. Dark theme, lime accents on the "them vs. you" contrast moments.
TARGET: The Quiet Competitor — 35–50, HVAC or landscaping, has a few regular clients and wants more, tracks which companies keep showing up on the same streets. Competitive without being loud about it. Primary objection: none explicit — they just haven't taken action yet. The trigger is status anxiety, not pain.
Dark theme — premium contrast
v28

Built for Cleaning Businesses

NICHE-SPECIFIC · CLEANING-FIRST · OUTCOME METRICS
"From booking call to invoice sent — without a single spreadsheet."
Cleaning-only copy: residential deep cleans, recurring clients, crew scheduling by neighbourhood, invoicing after each visit. Concrete numbers (avg invoice sent in 4 min, avg dispatch time 90s). No generic "field service" framing. Tests whether audience specificity outperforms broad positioning — highest signal experiment in the batch if cleaning traffic can be isolated.
TARGET: The Aspiring Operator — 28–42, residential cleaning business, 2–8 cleaners, running entirely on phone and WhatsApp. Wants to grow to 15 clients/week but can't manage the admin solo. Has Googled "scheduling app for cleaning business" at least once. Primary objection: "Is this actually made for cleaners or is it generic software they just renamed?"
White — approachable, practical
